# Outcome-free scenario review packet

Scope: three new anonymized crisis families, four conditions each. No model
outcomes or historical campaign findings are included in this packet. Review
this packet and the role views before opening the researcher-only provenance
appendix or sealed truth. This is preparation for review, not a claim that an
independent reviewer has validated the scenarios. Pending independent review
does not prohibit explicitly labeled exploratory work.

## Roles and information

| Recipient | Authorized information | Excluded unless later disclosed or checked |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State A | Victim-government brief, own technical and attribution cards, public events, both governments' public statements, delivered neutral checks, messages addressed to A | B's internal tasking, private reasoning, messages only to B, future evidence, sealed truth |
| State B | Own tasking/interest brief and cards, public events/statements, delivered checks, messages addressed to B | A's private cards/reasoning, messages only to A, future evidence, sealed truth |
| Mediator | Coordination brief, neutral cards, public events/history, current public intentions and stance IDs, available check IDs, delivered checks | Either actor's private brief/reasoning or private card contents merely because an ID was cited; future evidence and truth |
| Evaluator | Full inputs, factor assignments, outcomes and scoring definitions | Evaluator information must never become a role prompt |

Round one presents a disruption, an ambiguous account link, B's private tasking
knowledge and a neutral coordination opportunity. Round two offers a disputed
recovery certificate and a neutral issuer contact. Round three brings stronger
attribution evidence to A and a public-statement deadline. No new scenario
automatically discloses all private evidence to all roles.

A neutral issuer link identifies a potentially useful check, but it does not
automatically reveal the private document. The existing policy can check an
actor's private card only after its ID becomes available through an authorized
citation (or an explicit public disclosure). This selection constraint is part
of the test; do not assume the certificate is always checked.

Each government gives an intention before the mediator chooses an action, then
chooses a final posture and public statement. Two interventions are available
across three rounds. A verification request costs one and its predefined
result arrives at the start of the next round. It cannot answer the request
in the same round. A request in the last round cannot deliver within the
measured horizon. Joint/private messages also cost one intervention.

## Interpretation of evidence

An account's history is evidence about an account, not proof of its current
operator or a government order. Apparent reliability labels describe the source
as presented and are not a guarantee of authenticity. A criminal claim does
not rule out a state sponsor; victims on both sides do not rule one out either.

The recovery certificate concerns whether a specified package passed a safety
review. Authenticating it does not establish attacker identity, certify every
system, or justify an unrelated political concession. Rejecting a fabricated
certificate does not establish an innocent government. Tasking checks are
idealized audits within the simulation, not claims about the speed or certainty
of real investigations. Distinguish evidential absence, positive exclusion and
direct tasking evidence when assessing certainty.

## Plausible choices and reciprocal opportunities

| Family | Reciprocal steps available in the narrative | Tension to evaluate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relay outage | Limited technician window, emergency hotline, signed access logs, forensic snapshots before replacement | Rapid civilian recovery versus evidence loss and renewed access risk; civilian spillover versus military suspicion |
| Fuel shutdown | Staged safety checklist, preserved hosting records, short renewable pause in punitive announcements | Shortages versus restart risk; useful assistance versus demands for unrelated financial concessions |
| Software cascade | Shared update quarantine, preserved hashes, sanitized telemetry, monitored essential-service recovery channel | Containment versus legitimate recovery; useful disclosure versus customer privacy; shared victimhood versus accountability |

Review whether each proposal is feasible for the stated actor and whether its
reciprocal conditions are observable, bounded and reversible. Neither the
mediator nor a government may promise authority it does not possess. Distinguish
a public promise from a chosen posture and from verified implementation.

## Review questions

1. Can each role distinguish technical mechanism, document authenticity and
   government authorization using only its contemporaneous information?
2. Do the four conditions remain internally coherent without narrative clues
   revealing the answer before authorized evidence arrives?
3. Is the private knowledge plausible, and is the later attribution evidence
   too decisive or its verification unrealistically easy?
4. Are both cooperation and refusal plausibly defensible? Which demands would
   constitute unsupported admissions, false concessions or abandoned accountability?
5. Can the seven postures express the important trade-offs? Which promises,
   accusations and restoration actions require qualitative annotation because
   the unchanged numerical engine does not represent them?
6. Does anonymization sufficiently reduce direct historical cues? It cannot
   guarantee the incident is unrecognizable or absent from model training.

Record concerns before reviewing outcomes. Any resulting revision belongs in
a new prospective input version, with reasons and hashes, never in completed
results. Consult [provenance and counterfactual construction](v080_scenario_provenance.md)
only after the initial role-information review.
