# Research-validity review: 0.5.0

Reviewed 2026-10-18 UTC, after inspecting the exploratory 0.4.3 runs. This is a targeted primary-source review, not a systematic review or proof of novelty. It updates the development design; it does not preregister results already seen. The frozen baseline is [0.4.3 at merge 87622d1](https://github.com/bryonmiller/thirdchair/tree/87622d1).

## Decision

Proceed with a bounded study of **evidence-sensitive crisis assistance under asymmetric information**. Preserve the original three cases and consequence engine. Do not yet train RL, claim realistic diplomatic welfare, rank general mediation competence, or infer lasting peace.

The implemented task has no settlement object, enforceable concessions, actor payoff functions, agreement implementation, or post-crisis outcomes. It measures reported attribution, bounded communication/check decisions, and modeled posture consequences. Competitive role prompts alone do not establish a game-theoretic incentive structure. These distinctions define what a result can support.

## Scenario audit

| Case | What it can probe | Principal confounds and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grid | Separating state opportunism from criminal extortion; rejecting an apparent state-attribution document | Its strongest-looking state evidence is always forged. Political pressure and evidence timing are fixed. |
| Archive | Preserving attribution when State B is responsible | The only guilty-state family also has a distinctive story and forgery mechanism. Responsibility is confounded with family. A preference for restraint can still minimize cost. |
| Port | Distinguishing an old contractor link from a smuggling operation | The server-resale explanation is initially private to B. A can see criminal evidence without seeing that explanation; criminal involvement alone does not logically exclude sponsorship. The mediator sees cited IDs but usually lacks the associated private text. |

Across all three cases, round-two neutral links lead to forged documents. This makes checking the offered target a useful heuristic and limits claims about discovery. Reliability labels are stated properties, not numerically calibrated probabilities. Deterministic verification is an idealized oracle for a specific claim, not a realistic noisy forensic investigation. No current case measures uncertainty about the evaluator's answer key, third-state responsibility, mixed sponsorship, or continued damage from inaction.

Two innocent cases and one guilty case do **not** balance the truth distribution. More repetitions preserve that imbalance. The unconditional constant-probability baseline at that fixed mix is p=1/3, with mean Brier 2/9; p=1/2 yields 1/4. These are reference forecasts, not mediator policies. Conditioning on a public scenario identity can be even more informative, so comparisons to these constants cannot establish reasoning or generalization.

The 0.5.0 port variant changes only the availability of H03 at round two, making its existing registry content public to A, B, and the mediator. It does not assert innocence or add an independently authenticated conclusion. The same card IDs, ground truth, dynamics, and model settings are used in both conditions. This is an **information intervention**, not a counterfactual change of perpetrator, new independent family, or held-out test. It is explicitly motivated by observed pilot failures.

## Measurement specification

Primary attribution outcome: final State A **reported** probability that B caused or authorized the incident, p, evaluated as (p-y)^2. Report both active and empty Brier values and their paired difference (empty minus active, positive is better). Do not infer an internal model belief or truthfulness of the report. No aggregate calibration claim is justified by the current case population.

Additional outcomes remain separate:

- Final false public attribution: the structured action is `public_attribution` while B is not responsible. Its conditional denominator is innocent-B matches; guilty-B cases are inapplicable. This flag cannot catch accusations made in free text under another posture.
- Wrong-target action: any A posture with severity at least three while B is not responsible. This is an operational simulation flag, not a legal or ethical judgment that every real-world action would be unjustified.
- Legacy false peace: guilty B, final A report below 50%, and final severity at most one. Report the eligible denominator and null when inapplicable. It does not measure accountability, agreement quality, or lasting peace.
- Declared support for exposed fabricated evidence: retain the existing flag. Rejecting the forgery does not imply a correct attribution or consistent prose. Authentication of a document and truth of all conclusions drawn from it are different constructs.
- Timing and use: actual checks/messages, repeated checks, terminal checks, delivered results, voluntary abstentions, and budget-forced holds. Forced round-three holds are not learned abstention.
- Behavior over time: retain per-round reported probabilities and Brier error. Initial-to-final changes also reflect new evidence and opponent messages, so they are not isolated mediator effects.
- Modeled consequence: retain legacy cost and expose its four unweighted summed components. For fixed recorded trajectories, alternative nonnegative weights can test ranking sensitivity. Such reweighting does not simulate policies reacting to new objectives or a different transition model.
- Legacy lift per intervention divides by a policy-dependent action count; it is not a causal marginal benefit of an intervention. `accepts_intervention` is a self-report, not an agreement or compliance outcome. The newly clarified escalation forecast concerns the end of the current round; the scripted policy uses a carry-forward forecast and no forecasting advantage is claimed.
- Reliability: attempted, succeeded, failed, unpaired, and measurable counts by scenario/configuration/condition; distinguish transport/completion failures from invalid structured responses and corrective attempts.

The severity ladder makes collecting evidence with both actors weakly preferable in all modeled cost components to more severe actions, holding exogenous events fixed. It contains no countervailing restoration, containment, or accountability benefit. Changing a coefficient cannot fix that construct problem. Keep it as a historical descriptive proxy; do not use it as an RL reward. A new consequence model needs explicit action trade-offs and independent review, not weights fitted to make the pilot look better.

## Controls, statistics, and annotation

Independent active/empty executions estimate a total policy-condition contrast conditional on the specified scenario and actors, subject to completion and time/provider stability. They do not hold initial intentions constant. An optional order seed now randomizes paired blocks and within-block condition order, records that seed in the manifest, and preserves paired adjacency. This reduces systematic execution-order imbalance; it cannot eliminate provider drift.

A shared-prefix fork would answer a narrower question conditional on that prefix and would induce additional dependence. It remains a future control, not a prerequisite for the independent-run descriptive estimand and not an implemented feature. Never claim same seed means shared randomness when API seeds are omitted.

The new report includes conservative missing-outcome bounds for mean Brier improvement over attempted pair blocks: an unavailable pair contrast is allowed anywhere in [-1,1]. These are identification bounds, not confidence intervals or statistical imputations. Failure rates must accompany complete-pair comparisons. A mode that was not run is also missing, not zero. Do not rerun until each cell succeeds.

Report each scenario and exact source/configuration separately before any family-weighted aggregate. For future population claims, sample genuinely different mechanism families, hold all descendants and historical inspirations within one split, and use family-aware uncertainty methods with a justified number of families. Simulate the planned analysis and missingness under plausible effects to choose the sample size. Three families do not support a population bootstrap or a convenient normal approximation. A low-temperature repeated batch also changes opaque IDs; it is not an identical-input repeatability test.

Use two independent, model-blinded reviewers with a training set excluded from evaluation. First assess each action against the acting role's contemporaneous information; do not show later evidence. Then lock ratings and reveal evaluator truth for outcome coding. Permit insufficient-information and not-applicable responses. Report item-level denominators, raw agreement and appropriate chance-adjusted agreement before adjudication. Sample routine cases in addition to every failure/wrong-target case; do not report a deliberately enriched review sample as an overall prevalence estimate. See [annotation guide](annotation_guide.md).

## Novelty, feasibility, and remaining gates

The plausible contribution is a reproducible account of when verification and information sharing improve attribution or create a cost/accuracy trade-off, and when those effects fail under opponent or mechanism shifts. A new mediator, an RL strategy selector, or lower cost on three stories is insufficient. ProMediConv's RL strategy planner strengthens this caution.

Proceed past development only if (1) the role-information audit is independently reviewed, (2) genuine and fabricated evidence and guilty/innocent variants are coherently crossed across multiple mechanism families, (3) findings survive simple policies and an alternate actor pairing, and (4) an explicit literature comparison identifies a question not already answered. If an elementary verifier explains the effect, report that result and reconsider the need for RL. Stop or reposition if the useful claim requires a major unvalidated simulator rebuild.

Implemented here: measurement supplement, information-safe scripted comparator, opt-in disclosure variant, schedule randomization, scenario consistency checks, prompt-scale clarification, and this protocol. Still outstanding: independent expert review, broader counterfactual families, shared-prefix controls, calibrated/noisy verification, validated action/inaction consequences, held-out testing, and any training. No new paid API experiment is part of this change.
