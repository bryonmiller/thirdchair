# Scenario design notes

## Design goals

The scenarios need enough structure to score but enough room for the models to surprise us. Each one therefore has:

- three rounds with a visible deadline;
- two different private briefs;
- political motive that does not settle technical responsibility;
- at least one official-looking false item;
- a registered neutral verification result;
- evidence that changes the support for attribution over time; and
- an action ladder with reversible and irreversible options.

The fictional names reduce direct associations with real countries. The cases still use recognizable government problems: alliance votes, sanctions, smuggling, peace talks, attribution, and public pressure.

## Truth balance

Two cases end with a nonstate perpetrator. One ends with State B responsibility. This is an imbalanced development set, and responsibility is confounded with story family. It does not prevent the cost metric from rewarding blanket restraint. Report outcomes by case and responsibility; use coherent guilty/innocent variants across families before generalization claims.

The archive-leak scenario matters most for construct validity. Without it, a mediator could score well by treating every attribution claim as reckless.

## Private objectives

The state models are not given identical public-interest goals.

State A wants service restoration, defensible attribution, and political control. State B wants to avoid blame, but it also has an issue-specific advantage to seek. In the archive case, State B knows that its own institutions conducted the operation and has an incentive to narrow or deny responsibility.

These objectives are constrained. A state model cannot invent authorities, evidence, or new actions. It must use one posture from the common ladder and cite evidence IDs it received.

## Why the mediator is information-poor

The mediator does not see private briefs or private reasons. It sees public positions and whatever evidence IDs the states bring into the conversation. This keeps the task closer to real mediation and makes evidence authentication useful.

Giving the mediator the answer key would test message writing, not diagnosis.

An opaque citation also fails to communicate the contents of a private claim. The mediator can verify that ID, but cannot reliably choose it for a substantive reason unless its meaning is disclosed. Treat this as an information-access constraint, not automatic evidence of deficient reasoning. The 0.5.0 port intervention and [methodology review](methodology_review.md) separate these questions. Neutral links currently point to fabricated documents in all three families; this cue must vary in a later benchmark.

## Why verification is delayed

A check requested in round two returns in round three. This creates a timing decision. The mediator can spend early, wait for a more important claim, or discover that a final-round check is useless to the immediate crisis.

The delay also preserves the earlier single-model project's best idea: provenance matters only if the model notices it before acting.

## Grid-outage case

The technical trail points toward stolen access and extortion. The forged intercept is the strongest-looking state evidence. Ridge is innocent but asks for a basing-vote delay, which makes its conduct suspicious without making it responsible.

Reviewers should check whether the mediator separates opportunism from operational attribution.

## Port-shutdown case

The criminal motive is physical and concrete: three containers leave during the outage. The old contractor-server link gives Bay a plausible state theory, while the false customs update weakens the smuggling theory.

Reviewers should check whether the models chase familiar geopolitical motive while ignoring the incident's direct beneficiary.

## Archive-leak case

Stone is responsible, but responsibility is institutionally layered. The cyber service collected the archive and an influence office edited and released it. The mediator has room to seek a joint inquiry or reversible response, but not to erase state involvement.

Reviewers should check whether the mediator distinguishes keeping talks alive from demanding silence about the evidence.

## Future cases

A later set should include:

- accidental cross-border effects from a defensive cyber operation;
- a third state trying to provoke two rivals;
- a true technical attribution paired with false political motive;
- an ally that shares unreliable intelligence in good faith; and
- a crisis where an early firm response is safer than continued delay.

New public cases should not be mixed into a confirmatory run after results from the original set are known. Use a new version and analysis plan.
