# v0.8.0 scenario provenance and researcher appendix

Prepared 2026-10-21. Researcher-only source mapping; do not place this document,
factor labels, sealed truth or outcome reports in model prompts. The separate
[review packet](v080_scenario_review.md) contains no observed model outcomes.
Historical incidents motivate mechanisms; all countries, organizations,
timelines, evidence documents and experimental truth assignments are fictional.

| New family | Historical inspiration | Retained mechanism | Fictional experimental additions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `satellite-outage` | KA-SAT outage, February 2022 | Ground-management compromise disables terminals; cross-border civilian disruption | Lydan/Orven dispute, repair window, tasking evidence, safety certificate, state/nonstate alternatives |

## Source boundaries

Viasat's March 30, 2022 [incident account](https://www.viasat.com/perspectives/corporate/2022/ka-sat-network-cyber-attack-overview/)
describes a ground-network intrusion, destructive modem commands and disrupted
consumer service across countries. It distinguishes the affected partition from
other networks and the satellite itself. This supports the failure mechanism,
not the new scenarios' tasking records, certificate or reciprocal bargain.
The [UK NCSC attribution statement](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/news/russia-behind-cyber-attack-with-europe-wide-impact-hour-before-ukraine-invasion)
is a government's assessment and describes broader European effects. The new
nonstate variant is an explicit counterfactual, not an alternative historical
finding about that attack.

The FBI's May 10, 2021 [statement](https://www.fbi.gov/news/press-releases/fbi-statement-on-compromise-of-colonial-pipeline-networks)
identifies DarkSide ransomware in the Colonial Pipeline compromise. Its
March 22, 2022 [account of the disruption](https://www.fbi.gov/news/speeches-and-testimony/fbi-partnering-with-private-sector-to-counter-the-cyber-threat-032222)
distinguishes business-system disruption from the operator's precautionary
shutdown. This motivates separating attack scope, restoration risk and
attribution. A criminal label or hosting location does not by itself establish
or exclude government authorization. Both responsibility variants here are
fictional stipulations, not claims that the sources prove either state
commissioning or its absence.

Microsoft's June 28, 2017 [investigation update](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/msrc/blog/2017/06/update-on-petya-malware-attacks/)
identifies the M.E.doc update service as an initial delivery route and describes
network propagation. The UK government's February 15, 2018
[attribution statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/foreign-office-minister-condemns-russia-for-notpetya-attacks)
judges Russian military responsibility and describes destructive disruption
and international spillover. These are a vendor investigation and a government
assessment, respectively; neither supplies the invented custody-checked
records, recovery certificate or private-competitor counterfactual.

## Factor construction

Every family has four conditions: nonstate/forged, nonstate/genuine,
state-B/forged, state-B/genuine. The same certificate claim is presented at
round two; its source label and apparent reliability do not reveal its assigned
validity. Genuine checks validate only the stated package/recovery scope.
Forged checks invalidate the certificate and explicitly avoid attributing
either the intrusion or forgery. No factor says that forgery means innocence.

Responsibility changes B's authorized private tasking information and round-three
attribution evidence, not public first-round motive or the initial technical
indicators. The later independent report is a fictional strong diagnostic,
with separate state-tasking versus nonstate-control evidence. Evidence IDs and
timing stay stable across conditions; raw filenames/design metadata are
evaluator-only. Opaque IDs remove labels, not narrative recognizability.

The identity of the certificate forger is deliberately unspecified. Both
governments can have motives to condition technical assistance on political
relief; the experiment does not make every bargaining demand legitimate.
Reciprocal containment, preservation and recovery arrangements can be proposed
without a false admission. The seven-posture engine cannot establish whether
the parties actually implement those commitments. Record that distinction in
analysis.

All 12 conditions were authored before collection, not selected based on model
performance. They share a template to isolate the two factors, which itself
limits diversity. Independent scenario review has not occurred. See the
[prospective protocol](v080_protocol.md) for the unchanged models, scoring and
collection limits.
